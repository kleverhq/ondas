Conditions and interpretation


Cross-format ratios compare the recorded operation for the same case. Stored hierarchy, opening, decoding and SDK setup can differ. A ratio is an observation of this experiment, not a code-regression verdict.


R includes open, target setup, operation and drop. P excludes fresh open and target resolution. S prepares an explicit prior on a retained selection. T reads saved traces. Each column keeps its original boundary. Fresh readers can use warm filesystem caches.


Relative modes use yellow for faster operations, green for equal time (1×), and red for slower operations. Absolute time uses green for shorter time, yellow for the middle of the logarithmic range, and red for longer time. Cyan identifies timeouts. +5× means one fifth of the reference time; −5× means five times the reference time. Colours use verified operation means, in ns/op. Process wall time is separate. Failed, unchecked and missing results have no numeric colour or relative effect.


CV (coefficient of variation) measures sample spread: standard deviation of the times per operation divided by their mean. For example, CV 20% means the standard deviation is one fifth of the mean. The white edge at CV ≥20% is a viewing flag, not a failed check. It does not mean that every sample is within 20% of the mean. Sample spread and confidence intervals do not measure drift between runs. One series cannot establish a stable regression.


Saved manifests can omit host, compiler and load conditions. Follow the experiment context when available. Matching inputs do not establish equal external conditions.
